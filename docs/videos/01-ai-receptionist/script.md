## Scene 1: Hook (0:00–0:20)

**Visual:** Night city. A phone lights up: a WhatsApp message, "Hi, are you open
tomorrow? I need a quote." Clock reads 3:02 A.M. The shop is dark. The message
sits unread, and a second business's reply pops up instead.

**Narration:**
> [curious] It's three in the morning, and a customer just messaged your business.
> [pause] Who answers?
> [matter-of-fact] If the answer is "nobody until nine," that customer is probably
> already talking to someone else.
> [warmly] In the next few minutes, I'll show you exactly how an AI receptionist
> answers that message, and what separates a good one from an annoying one.

## Scene 2: The problem (0:20–0:45)

**Visual:** A 24-hour ring split into "open" (gold, 9–5) and "closed" (navy, the
rest). Messages land on the navy side as dots and pile up. Labels: website chat,
WhatsApp, phone.

**Narration:**
> Most small businesses are open about forty hours a week.
> Customers message all one hundred and sixty-eight.
> [pause] Evenings, weekends, other time zones. And every channel is different:
> your website, WhatsApp, the phone.
> An AI receptionist sits on all of them at once, and never goes off shift.

## Scene 3: The four parts (0:45–2:30)

**Visual:** One diagram builds up across this scene:
`Channels → Brain (LLM) ← Knowledge base` with `→ Actions (lead to inbox)` at the
end. Each block lights up as it's explained.

### 3a. Channels (0:45–1:05)

**Visual:** Three icons slide in (chat bubble, WhatsApp, microphone) and merge into
one pipe.

> [steady] Part one: the channels.
> This is wherever your customers already are: a chat window on your website, a
> voice button in the browser, your WhatsApp number.
> The trick is that all of them feed one single agent, so the answer is the same
> wherever the customer asks.

### 3b. The brain (1:05–1:30)

**Visual:** The pipe enters a glowing core labeled "Language model". Text in, text out.

> Part two: the brain. That's a large language model, the same kind of AI behind
> the chat assistants you've already used.
> It's great at understanding messy, real human messages, [lightly] typos and all.
> [serious] But on its own, it knows nothing about *your* business. Ask it your
> opening hours, and it'll happily guess.

### 3c. The knowledge base (1:30–2:05)

**Visual:** Documents (price list, FAQ, opening hours, services) drop into a
library block. A question arrives, the matching paragraph highlights and flies into
the brain, then the answer comes out. Small label: "RAG: retrieval-augmented generation".

> [pause] Which is why part three matters most: the knowledge base.
> You give the agent your own information: services, prices, hours, policies.
> When a question comes in, the system first finds the few passages that actually
> answer it, and hands only those to the brain.
> [explaining] Engineers call this retrieval-augmented generation, R-A-G.
> [warmly] In plain words: the AI looks it up before it speaks.
> That's the difference between "I think we're open Sundays" and "We're open
> Sundays, ten to four."

### 3d. Actions (2:05–2:30)

**Visual:** The conversation becomes a lead card (name, phone, request, time) that
flies into an email inbox with a ping.

> Part four: actions. Answering questions is nice, but the goal is the lead.
> A good receptionist collects the customer's name, what they need, and how to
> reach them, and sends it straight to the owner's inbox.
> [satisfied] You wake up to booked conversations, not missed ones.

## Scene 4: Good vs annoying (2:30–3:05)

**Visual:** Split screen. Left "Annoying" (red): looping bot, made-up answer, no
way to reach a human. Right "Good" (green): answers from the knowledge base, says
"I'll pass this to the team", hands over the lead.

> [candid] Now, we've all met a bad chatbot. So here's the checklist.
> One: it answers only from your information, and says "I don't know" instead of
> guessing.
> Two: it hands off to a human for anything it shouldn't decide.
> Three: it captures the lead every time, not just the conversation.
> [pause] Get those three right, and customers barely notice they're talking to an AI.
