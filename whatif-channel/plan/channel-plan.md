# Channel plan

## Why the reference channel works
- **The question is the hook.** "What if you were the last guy on Earth?" makes a promise in the title alone.
  The best ones are personal ("you"), extreme ("last", "only", "8 billion"), and easy to picture.
- **Simple characters, big emotion.** Faceless mascots with exaggerated expressions read instantly at phone size.
- **Cheap visuals that keep moving.** Stills change every 4–5 s with slow push, pan and pull moves. No real animation is needed.
- **Thumbnails:** 2–3 huge outlined words plus one character showing a strong emotion. The thumbnail adds to the title rather than repeating it.
- **Length:** 7–8 min. Long enough for mid-roll-free watch time, short enough to finish.

## Our version
- **Look:** flat cut-paper pebble people, colourful, tiny dot eyes (see `brand/style.txt`). It should be recognisable
  in one frame and clearly not a copy.
- **Cast:** Pip (teal, yellow scarf), the "you" of every episode, and Momo (coral, glasses), the calm neighbour.
  Add characters only when a series needs them.
- **Voice:** one warm narrator voice (Gemini TTS "Iapetus") in every episode.
- **Script shape (8 min, about 1,150 words):**
  1. 0:00–0:30: the question, the stakes and a number ("5.5 billion people…"), then "so what actually happens?"
  2. Rules of the hypothetical in one breath, so commenters can't pick holes in it.
  3. A time ladder: day 1, week 1, month 1, month 6, the end. Every chapter adds a new problem.
  4. One real-world anchor per chapter (a real event or statistic), with a source in notes.md.
  5. Emotional turn near the end ("what we found when we looked up").
  6. A comment prompt plus a teaser for the next question.

## Rules
- Every factual number goes in the episode's `notes.md` with a source before publishing. No invented statistics.
- No real people, brands or logos in images. Generic devices only.
- Original topics. Don't remake a competitor's exact title. Use our own angle on evergreen questions.
- Keep YouTube's "inauthentic content" policy in mind: each episode needs real research and a fresh script.
  A template filled with filler gets demonetized. Don't publish more often than quality allows. Aim for 1–2 a week.
